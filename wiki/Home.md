# Gregory's Awesome Teams

This is the handbook for your project. You are building a product or service and
demonstrating it live on **December 1st, 2026**, using the IBM Bob harness.

**New here? Go straight to [Getting Started](Getting-Started).** It takes about
ten minutes and you do not need to understand anything first.

---

## The one idea behind all of this

You are not learning "how to use AI". You are learning three skills that decide
whether the AI is useful to you or not:

| Skill | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **[Prompt engineering](Prompt-Engineering)** | What do I say? |
| **[Context engineering](Context-Engineering)** | What can it see while I say it? |
| **[Harness engineering](Harness-Engineering)** | What machinery is around it? |

Most people only ever do the first one, badly, and conclude the AI is not very
good. The gap between a frustrating session and a productive one is almost
always the second and third.

The animated diagram on [the repository front page](https://github.com/techcwbldr26/bob-scripts-gregorys-awesome-teams)
shows this in 24 seconds. Watch it once.

## What good looks like, at a glance

By the deadline you should be able to answer these without hesitating:

1. **Who is this for, specifically?** Not "students" — which students, doing what,
   failing how today.
2. **How do you know it works?** A number from an eval run, with how many cases.
3. **What evidence says this is the right thing to build?** A link and a date.
4. **What does it not do?** Said with confidence, not embarrassment.
5. **What happens when it fails in front of us?** A rehearsed answer.

If you can answer all five, the demo takes care of itself. The six stages in
[The Build Flow](The-Build-Flow) exist to produce those five answers.
